*Draft 2026-08-13. Supersedes `website-copy.md`, which led with voice capture and privacy — both
retired. This leads with the surfaces outside the app, and pays off with the room behind them.*

*Voice unchanged: lowercase for Fil's own words, spare, calm, gently contrarian. `[bracketed notes]`
are direction, not copy.*

## The three pillars

1. **the door** — what you're working on is already out there, on six surfaces that aren't the app.
2. **the form** — every thought arrives as its own object. unchosen, unrepeatable.
3. **the room** — and when you do go in, it's somewhere worth staying.

## What changed from v1, and why

- **Lead is the door, not voice capture.** The surfaces are the differentiator; the beauty is the
  reward behind them.
- **The unit is a folder, not a note.** You pin the thing you're in the middle of.
- **Privacy is no longer the wedge** — the cloud pivot broke that claim. It survives as an honest
  footnote, not a pillar.
- **"No app to open" is retired as a claim.** It isn't true: you glance from outside, but you open
  Fil to read, add, and rearrange. The honest version is that the app is *already there* when you
  context-switch — you never open it just to check.
- **Two factual corrections.** Titles come from the first line of the fil, not from on-device
  intelligence. And folders exist now, so v1's "no folders" line is gone.

## 3. The turn — what fil isn't

> ### you shouldn't have to go looking for your own head
>
> every other app puts your things behind a launch, a search, and a folder tree. so you file
> something carefully and never see it again.
>
> fil works the other way round. what you're working on lives where you already look forty times a

>
> no inbox to clear. no streaks. no tags to maintain. no system to keep up with.

## 4. The form

> ### no two thoughts look alike
>
> every fil arrives with its own colour, its own shape, its own gradient. you don't pick it and you
> can't change it. it's given — the way the thought was.
>
> other apps hand you an identical grey row and ask you to do the work: tag it, colour it, title it,
> file it. fil hands you something that already looks like itself.
>
> so you know a thought on sight. you don't read your folder, you recognise it.
>
> and it accrues. after six months your canvas isn't a longer list — it's a landscape of distinct
> things you made.

`[Visual: many blobs at once. A wall of them. The point is that none of them repeat.]`

## Notes for build

- **The hero image is the pitch.** Expanded Dynamic Island first, lock screen second. Never an app
  screen in the hero slot — that's the shot every competitor already uses.
- **State the no-customisation stance proudly.** "you can't change the colours" will draw review
  requests for a colour picker. Said as a stance it self-selects the right audience; discovered as a
  gap it reads as unfinished.
- **Claim guardrails:**
  - **Don't write "no app to open."** You glance from outside; you open Fil to read, add, and
    rearrange. Say "already there when you context-switch."
  - **Don't write "always on your lock screen"** until the accessory widget ships — Live Activities
    are capped around 8 hours, and a second activity can push ours off the single slot.
  - **Titles come from the first line** of the fil. Do not claim on-device intelligence writes them.
  - **Transcription is on-device where the device supports it** — never "100% on-device."
  - **No superlatives.** We could not verify that nobody else pins a folder to the lock screen.
    Describe what it does and let it be obviously new.
- **Type / colour / motion:** unchanged from v1 — DM Sans, near-monochrome canvas, blobs carry all
  the colour, slow organic motion.

## Open, before this ships

- **The accessory (lock-screen) widget doesn't exist yet** — `FilPinnedWidget` is `systemSmall`
  only. Until it does, the lock-screen surface is Live-Activity-only and therefore time-bounded.
  The page's central claim depends on it.
- `AppStore/metadata.md` — full rewrite against this positioning.
- Legal pages and the hosted site — both still carry July's privacy-led language.
